The problem

In apartment communities, maintenance problems reach the team through WhatsApp, calls, security staff, workers and face-to-face talks. The issue is not that residents cannot report a problem. There is no single reliable flow for capturing it, setting priority, assigning responsibility, tracking progress and sharing the outcome.

The result: residents follow up repeatedly, staff get incomplete or duplicate information, and managers cannot see unresolved or recurring issues.

Six pain points

Fragmented reporting

Different residents use different channels, so requests are hard to consolidate.

Duplicate complaints

One broken lift is reported by many people, creating noise.

Weak prioritization

Urgent and cosmetic issues share one stream with no priority signal.

Missing ownership

A complaint can exist with nobody responsible for the next step.

No status visibility

Residents cannot tell if an issue is new, assigned, in progress or resolved.

No usable history

Recurring problems are hard to spot without a structured record.

Root of the problem

The visible complaint is "my issue is taking too long". The underlying problem is scattered communication: incomplete information, unclear priority and ownership, weak visibility, repeated follow-ups, and frustration for everyone.

The problem is the lack of a trustworthy system between reporting an issue and knowing it is being handled.
